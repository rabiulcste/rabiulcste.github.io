Abstract

Diffusion and flow models are effective world models for visual reinforcement learning, letting agents learn behaviors in imagination. In current diffusion-based online agents, the world model serves as a black-box simulator. A separate policy network learns from its generated rollouts, so the diffusion model's backbone representations go unused for control. We introduce DRIFT, an agent whose policy learns entirely in imagination on the same Flow Transformer backbone as its world model, trained online from scratch. Three modeling choices make this work. First, denoising alone yields a less effective control state for the actor–critic. We therefore train the backbone to predict the next frame's latent from the control state's summary of the history and the current action, a reconstruction-free objective that makes the control state predictive and improves control. Second, the world model's reward predictions are less accurate on the generated latents than on the real ones. A simple and effective mitigation is to also train the world model on its own rollouts, with targets from the real data. Third, shortcut flow matching reduces imagination time to one denoising step per imagined transition while matching multi-step sampling in rollout accuracy. Imagining in a compact continuous latent space, DRIFT uses 33× fewer FLOPs per imagined transition than prior diffusion-based agents. It outperforms diffusion and flow baselines on Craftium and on Crafter with full-scale rewards, and is competitive on Atari. These results show that one diffusion generative backbone can both imagine and control in online RL.
