TL;DR: A diffusion model at two levels. One network denoises a compact embedding and the pixels, so it learns visual representations and generates images without labels, trained by denoising alone.

Representations learned by reconstructing, denoising or generating the image still trail those of non-generative self-supervised learning (SSL) methods. An image's exact color and framing are stochastic details that vary among images with the same content. A representation trained to reproduce the image is pushed to encode these details. Recognition, in contrast, benefits from invariance to them.

A diffusion model at two levels

DEM generates the representation and the image separately, within one network and with one denoising objective. In representation space, it denoises the embedding of an image given another view of the same image. In pixel space, it generates the image that this embedding describes. The network is one shared backbone, a Transformer, with two small heads on it. The embedding denoiser works at the embedding level, and the pixel head works at the pixel level.

Training

Each training step takes three views of one image. The teacher, an exponential moving average (EMA) of the network, embeds a clean view into the target. At the embedding level, the backbone reads the noised view, and the embedding denoiser recovers the target from a noisy copy of it (loss 1). At the pixel level, the backbone reads a noisy copy of the image with the target as its condition, and the pixel head predicts the image (loss 2).

Inference

After training, the teacher is no longer used. The Vision Transformer's features of an image are its representation, and no head is needed. To generate without labels, the embedding denoiser first samples an embedding from noise with no view, and the Vision Transformer and pixel head then draw the image given it. Given only a crop, the embedding denoiser samples several embeddings that fit the crop, and the pixel head draws one image for each.

Noise-map views

Masking and cropping amount to full noise on some patches. A single noising process, which sets a noise level for each patch, can thus produce both the views of SSL and the noisy images of diffusion. DEM draws all its inputs from this process, so its two tasks train on the same inputs. In training, the student sees a noised view (noise combined with a mask), the teacher a clean view, and the pixel pass a noised image (the whole image at one noise level).

Asymmetric noise schedules

The embedding is denoised mostly near full noise, close to the SSL task where semantics are learned, and the pixels at lower noise, where the stochastic details are drawn.

The full-noise special case

Conceptually, joint-embedding SSL methods are a special case of DEM. These methods train a network to predict the embedding of one view of an image from another view. Suppose this network also receives a noisy copy of the embedding it predicts. If the copy is pure noise, it carries no information, and the task is unchanged. Prediction is thus view-conditioned embedding denoising at full noise. At lower noise, DEM learns the distribution that prediction reduces to its mean.

One embedding, many images

The embedding sets the content of the image, and the pixel noise supplies the stochastic details it leaves out.

Completion and in-painting

DEM completes crops and in-paints without training for either.
